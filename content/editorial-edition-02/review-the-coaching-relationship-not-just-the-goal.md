A coaching relationship can continue smoothly while important questions remain unasked. The sessions happen. The client participates. The coach prepares. Yet neither person has explicitly checked whether the arrangement is still useful.

A review gives the relationship permission to change. It can preserve what works, expose an uncomfortable mismatch or help conclude the engagement responsibly. That is part of serving the client, not an admission that the original agreement failed.

The [ICF Code of Ethics](https://coachingfederation.org/ethics/code-of-ethics/) addresses professional responsibilities within the coaching relationship. The review conversation below is a practical way to keep the working arrangement understandable as circumstances evolve.

## Set the expectation before a concern appears

Agree early that the engagement will be reviewed. Explain that the client can discuss the process, raise concerns and reconsider fit. A review should not arrive as a surprise examination of whether the client has progressed enough.

Choose a point that suits the engagement, such as an agreed milestone or a small number of sessions. Leave room to revisit sooner if the goal, sponsor or circumstances change.

Also explain how concerns can be raised outside that meeting. A client should not have to wait for a formal checkpoint to say that the format is inaccessible, a boundary is unclear or a discussion is not serving their needs.

## Ask about usefulness in specific terms

“Are you happy with the coaching?” can invite a polite answer. Ask what has been useful, what has been difficult to use and where the client would like the work to change.

Invite an example. A fictional client might say that reflecting on a recent conversation helped them identify a clearer next step, while between-session exercises have been too large for their current workload. That gives both people something concrete to examine.

Do not immediately defend the method. First check that you understand the feedback. The client is describing their experience; you can explore it without agreeing with every interpretation or treating it as a verdict on your professional identity.

## Review the working conditions as well as the goal

The focus may still matter while the arrangement needs adjustment. Inspect the practical elements that shape the work.

| Area | Review question |
| --- | --- |
| Focus | Is this still the question the client wants to work on? |
| Format | Does the session length, pace and medium support the work? |
| Participation | What preparation or follow-up is useful and realistic? |
| Boundaries | Are roles, information handling and contact expectations clear? |
| Fit | Is this coach and approach still appropriate for the need? |

If a sponsor is involved, follow the agreed information-sharing arrangement. A review should not become an informal expansion of what the sponsor can access.

## Distinguish change from attribution

A client may report a better conversation, a new role or a business improvement during the engagement. Celebrate what they value while remaining careful about what caused it.

Many influences can contribute: their own work, colleagues, training, circumstances and opportunities outside the sessions. A testimonial describes an experience; it is not an independent demonstration that coaching produced the whole outcome.

If feedback may later be used publicly, discuss that separately and obtain appropriate permission. Do not make the review feel like a request for marketing material disguised as reflection.

## Decide whether to continue, adjust or conclude

Continuing should be a choice with a clear reason. Identify the focus of the next phase and preserve the parts of the arrangement that are useful.

Adjusting may involve a narrower goal, a different rhythm or clearer expectations. Record the change so both people know what has been agreed. If the need falls outside the coach's scope or the fit is poor, discuss suitable next steps without pressuring the client to stay.

When concluding, give the client room to reflect on what they want to carry forward. Clarify any remaining practical matters and how records or access will be handled according to the agreement.

A strong coaching relationship can tolerate a question about its usefulness. Make that question explicit, listen without rushing to reassurance and let the answer influence what happens next. The client deserves an engagement that remains a deliberate choice.
