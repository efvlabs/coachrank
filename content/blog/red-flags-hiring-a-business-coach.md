Anyone can call themselves a business coach. There is no licence to lose, no register to be struck from, and no exam that has to be passed. Most people working in the field are serious about it — but the absence of a gate means the market rewards confidence at least as much as competence.

These are the signals worth acting on.

## 1. A guaranteed business outcome

Revenue, funding, a promotion, a valuation. None of these depend only on the coaching, so none of them can honestly be guaranteed. A guarantee is a marketing decision, and it tells you what will be prioritised when the two conflict.

## 2. Pressure that only exists in the sales conversation

Expiring discounts, "one place left", a price that rises if you think about it overnight. Urgency is a legitimate tool for selling commodities. For a months-long relationship built on trust, it is evidence that the sale matters more than the fit.

## 3. No answer to "who does badly with you?"

Every experienced practitioner knows who they cannot help. "Anyone can benefit from coaching" is a slogan, not an answer, and it usually means the person has not worked with enough people to see the pattern.

## 4. Credentials doing all the talking

Certifications in coaching vary from serious multi-year supervision to a weekend and an invoice, and the logos look similar. A coach who leads with badges rather than with a description of the work is inviting you to evaluate the wrong thing.

## 5. Scope that keeps expanding

Coaching that drifts into financial advice, medical or mental-health territory, or legal opinion is a real risk — not because the person is malicious, but because those fields are regulated for reasons. A good coach names the boundary before you have to.

## 6. Testimonials without specifics

"Transformational." "Life-changing." Compare with: "We went from three founders arguing about everything to a defined decision process in about four months." The second is checkable and the first is not, and the difference is not writing skill.

## 7. Vagueness about money

Not the price — the terms. What is included, what notice applies, what happens if it stops working. Discomfort with commercial specifics tends to be a general trait rather than a local one.

## The one that is not a red flag

**A coach who will not promise results.**

This reads as weakness and is the opposite. Careful language about outcomes usually means someone has thought about what they can and cannot control, and has decided not to sell you the part they cannot. It is the most under-rated positive signal in the field.
