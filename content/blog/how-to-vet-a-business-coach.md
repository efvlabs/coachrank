Search for a business coach and you will find lists. Top 10 coaches. Best executive coaches of the year. Award winners, certified members, featured partners.

Almost all of it is paid for somewhere in the chain — through a membership fee, an application fee, an advertising slot, or an affiliate arrangement on the other side. That is not a scandal. It is how directories have always worked. The problem is that most of them present it as editorial judgement.

Once you assume a list is paid, it becomes useful again. You just have to read it as an advertisement, which is what it is.

## What a paid position actually tells you

More than nothing, and less than a recommendation.

A coach who pays to be visible is spending money to be found. That tells you they are actively taking clients, that they have budget, and that they are confident enough in their conversion to buy attention. Those are real facts about a business.

What it does not tell you: whether they are any good, whether they are right for you, or whether the fee is fair. Money buys placement. It does not buy competence, and no honest directory should imply otherwise.

## The four checks worth doing

**1. Look for specificity, not credentials.** Coaching has no protected title and no single meaningful licence, so certifications vary enormously in what they required. Far more informative is whether a coach can describe precisely who they work with. "I work with founders of 10-50 person software companies going through their first management layer" is a real answer. "I help ambitious leaders unlock their potential" is not an answer at all.

**2. Ask for two clients who left.** Anyone can produce a happy testimonial. Ask instead about people who finished, or who stopped early, and what happened. A coach who can talk plainly about a relationship that ended — and why — is telling you they have a real practice and a realistic view of it.

**3. Check what they claim about outcomes.** Coaching cannot guarantee revenue, funding, or a promotion, because none of those depend only on the coaching. A guarantee of a business result is a marketing decision, not a professional one. Careful language about outcomes is a good sign, not a weak one.

**4. Have the money conversation early.** Not just the fee — the terms. What is included, what happens between sessions, what notice applies, what happens if it stops working. A coach who is comfortable being specific about commercial terms will usually be comfortable being specific about everything else.

## The one thing a directory can honestly give you

A directory cannot tell you who is good. It can tell you who is findable, who is active, and — if it is honest — what they paid to be there.

That last one is the part almost nobody publishes. A ranking that hides its own mechanism is asking you to mistake advertising for assessment. A ranking that shows you the price is at least handing you the information you need to discount it correctly.
