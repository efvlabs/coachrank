## Reddit: positioning exercise

Title: A positioning exercise for solo businesses: make the deliverable specific

Body:

I'm building CoachRank, and one exercise in our latest guide is to separate the customer, offer and reason to choose it.

Start with four lines:

- Who is in the situation you can help with?
- What exactly do they receive?
- What alternative would they otherwise use?
- What difference can you support with evidence?

Here's a fictional example. A designer writes, “I help businesses stand out.” That leaves the buyer doing a lot of interpretation. A more useful working version might be, “I create a five-page website for independent consultants launching their first fixed-scope service, using their existing copy and photography.”

That still needs evidence and a reason to choose the designer. But now a prospect can question the scope, timing and fit. That's progress you can test.

Give your statement to someone in the intended audience. Ask them to explain what they think they would receive and what remains unclear. Record their words before explaining your intention.

Which part of describing your offer is hardest: choosing the audience, stating the deliverable, or explaining the difference?

## Reddit: audit exercise

Title: Before sharing a paid offer, walk through these six checks

Body:

I'm building CoachRank and published a small-business brand audit worksheet. This is the core exercise, which you can do without a tool.

Choose one offer and follow the path a stranger would take from first impression to buying.

1. Audience: can they recognize that the offer is relevant to their situation?
2. Offer: can they name the deliverable, price or quote process, and next step?
3. Consistency: do your profile, landing page and checkout describe the same thing?
4. Evidence: does the proof support the specific promise, with demonstrations labeled honestly?
5. Buying path: does the button work, and is the final price understandable?
6. Delivery: have you checked how the buyer receives what they paid for?

For each check, write: what I saw, what is unclear or broken, and the next action. Use “not checked” when you don't have evidence. An assumption is not a pass.

Address broken buying or delivery steps first. Then pick one important point of confusion to investigate. You don't need to redesign the entire brand to begin.

This doesn't predict sales. It helps you find avoidable problems before you promote an offer.

What has a real customer misunderstood about your page or buying process?

## Publication sequence and what to learn

Begin with one exercise post. Respond to the actual questions it receives. Publish the sample-report demonstration as a separate post so buyers can see the paid result. Adapt the next post to the objections and language people use. Check Studio's Buying journey page for source, product, checkout, confirmed-payment and report counts.

If relevant readers never reach the product, inspect the transition and offer relevance. If checkout starts but payment does not follow, inspect price, trust and checkout behavior. If people pay but do not finish, inspect delivery and the assessment experience. Small counts are observations, not stable conversion benchmarks.
